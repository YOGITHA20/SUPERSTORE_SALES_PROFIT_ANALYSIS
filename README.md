# Superstore Sales & Profit Analysis

## 📌 Project Overview

This project analyzes Superstore sales and profitability to identify
the major drivers of profit, loss-making areas, and the impact of
discounting on business performance.

The project follows an end-to-end Business Analyst workflow:

**Excel → SQL → Power BI**

---

## 🎯 Business Problem

The business wants to understand:

- Which categories and sub-categories drive profitability?
- Which products are generating losses?
- How does discounting affect profitability?
- Which regions and customer segments perform best?
- Where are the major opportunities for improving profit?

---

## 🛠️ Tools Used

- **Excel** – Data cleaning and preparation

---

## 📊 Key Findings

### Overall Performance

- Total Sales: **$2.30M**
- Total Profit: **$286.40K**
- Overall Profit Margin: **12.47%**
- Total Unique Orders: **5,009**

### Profitability

- Technology is the strongest category by total profit.
- Several sub-categories generate negative profit despite generating sales.
- Tables has a negative profit margin of **-8.56%**.

### Discount Impact

Profitability deteriorates significantly at higher discount levels.
The analysis shows losses beginning around the **30% discount level**.

### Regional Performance

The West region generates the highest total profit among the four
regions.

### Product Performance

The analysis identifies both highly profitable products and products
that generate significant losses, allowing management to focus on
product-level profitability rather than sales alone.

---

## 💡 Business Recommendations

1. Review high-discount pricing strategies, particularly discounts
   around and above 30%.

2. Investigate consistently loss-making products and evaluate their
   pricing, cost structure, and discount levels.

3. Protect and promote high-margin products and sub-categories.

4. Analyze weak region-category combinations instead of evaluating
   regions only at an overall level.

5. Avoid judging product performance using sales alone; profitability
   should be considered alongside revenue.
